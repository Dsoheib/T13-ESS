# Cover letter — Computers & Security

Dear Editors,

We submit "When Confidence Intervals Assume Independence: Dependence-Aware Intervals for Intrusion-Detection Evaluation" for consideration as an original research article.

Dependence of the alarm indicator widens a qualifying false-positive interval on the clean Monday CICIDS2017 prefix, and it changes one close Wednesday ranking. It does not overturn the large detector gaps. The intervals are metric-specific: an ESS-adjusted Wilson plug-in for the false-positive rate, an ESS plug-in on the attack subsequence for recall, and a moving-block bootstrap of the Mann–Whitney statistic for AUC. At n=20000 in the strongest short-memory cell, those intervals cover near or below nominal. Sweeping the Hurst exponent over five values locates where the short-memory procedure stops working rather than merely exhibiting a failure at a point: ESS-Wilson holds to H=0.6 (0.93) and degrades monotonically above it, to 0.82, 0.55 and 0.33, while the effective sample size the reporting rule already asks for falls from 1087 to 64. On a resampled Monday Flow Duration trace the false-positive plug-in improves on the nominal-n interval and remains short of 0.95 at n=5000. The reporting rule is one sentence: use ESS–Wilson for a short-memory false-positive rate when the autocorrelation estimators agree and n_eff is at least 50; otherwise report n_eff and no interval.

The screened corpus illustrates reporting practice, with its two denominators stated separately: 20 of 31 screened records resolved far enough to code which estimand their reported uncertainty targets, and the narrower frame that also requires a time-aware split resolved 5 of 16. We make no prevalence claim on either. The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. Generative-AI involvement is disclosed per your policy, and all authors have approved the submission. We suggest reviewers with expertise in network-measurement statistics, resampling for dependent data, and machine-learning-for-security evaluation methodology.

Sincerely,
Soheib Cherih (corresponding author), on behalf of all authors
